# deafmode operational states

---

# purpose

Operational states represent the current coherence, capacity, and directional integrity of the system.

They are not emotional labels.

They are operational abstractions used for:
- telemetry
- inference
- automation
- coordination
- recovery management

States should remain:
- low-noise
- semantically stable
- operationally meaningful

---

# STABLE

## definition

System resources are balanced and operational coherence is maintained.

## indicators

- predictable execution
- manageable stress load
- stable recovery
- sustainable work cadence
- low fragmentation

## common characteristics

- tasks complete without excessive switching
- interruptions remain recoverable
- planning horizon remains intact

## avoid

- unnecessary escalation
- reactive overload
- artificial optimization pressure

## likely transitions

- FOCUSED
- RECOVERING
- OVERLOADED

---

# RECOVERING

## definition

System coherence returning after overload, fragmentation, or degradation.

## indicators

- improving recovery quality
- reduced volatility
- restored execution capacity
- lower task-switch frequency
- increasing resilience

## common characteristics

- selective engagement
- deliberate pacing
- reduced tolerance for noise

## avoid

- overcommitment
- novelty seeking
- excessive stimulation
- reactive work environments

## likely transitions

- STABLE
- FOCUSED
- FRAGMENTED

---

# OVERLOADED

## definition

Operational demand exceeds sustainable capacity.

## indicators

- cognitive saturation
- degraded prioritization
- prolonged recovery demand
- increasing backlog pressure
- elevated switching cost

## common characteristics

- execution becomes reactive
- planning collapses into urgency
- interruptions become destabilizing

## avoid

- additional commitments
- parallel obligations
- context-heavy work
- escalation behavior

## likely transitions

- DEGRADED
- FRAGMENTED
- RECOVERING

---

# FRAGMENTED

## definition

Attention and execution coherence degraded by excessive switching or interruption.

## indicators

- shallow task cycling
- reduced completion rates
- unstable focus
- excessive interruption exposure
- high coordination overhead

## common characteristics

- difficulty sustaining deep work
- low continuity between tasks
- elevated mental noise

## avoid

- notification-heavy environments
- multitasking
- fragmented scheduling
- excessive communication load

## likely transitions

- OVERLOADED
- DEGRADED
- RECOVERING

---

# FOCUSED

## definition

High coherence and sustained directional execution.

## indicators

- uninterrupted work blocks
- low switching cost
- strong task continuity
- high execution clarity
- efficient resource utilization

## common characteristics

- reduced reactive behavior
- increased completion velocity
- improved strategic thinking

## avoid

- unnecessary interruption
- impulsive context changes
- overextension beyond recovery limits

## likely transitions

- STABLE
- OVERLOADED
- FRAGMENTED

---

# DEGRADED

## definition

Operational integrity compromised by fatigue, instability, or prolonged overload.

## indicators

- inconsistent execution
- elevated recovery demand
- unstable cadence
- reduced resilience
- impaired prioritization

## common characteristics

- shortened planning horizon
- increased operational friction
- fragile recovery behavior

## avoid

- high-risk decisions
- expansion efforts
- excessive obligations
- unstable environments

## likely transitions

- RECOVERING
- FRAGMENTED
- OVERLOADED

---

# state transition principles

## transitions should be conservative

States should not oscillate rapidly from transient fluctuations.

Prefer:
- directional stability
- trend awareness
- recovery continuity

over:
- reactive classification

---

## confidence matters

State inference should tolerate ambiguity.

Low-confidence conditions should:
- reduce escalation
- preserve stability
- avoid false certainty

---

## operational priority hierarchy

When conflicts exist:

1. integrity
2. recovery
3. coherence
4. output
5. optimization

---

# non-goals

Operational states are not intended to represent:
- identity
- personality
- self-worth
- emotion classification
- productivity aesthetics

They exist solely to describe operational system conditions.
